Octopus Cards Limited says it detected a cyberattack on the morning of November 27 and immediately acted to contain the incident. Its initial assessment involves **about 7,500 customers’ personal information**, including names and photographs. For some, automatic add-value service information may also be involved: bank codes and automatic add-value amounts. The company says credit-card or bank-account information is not involved; the separately named bank codes should not be misreported as bank-account numbers.

A separate group consists of **about 22,000 merchants’ general contact information**: company contact names, positions, email addresses and telephone numbers. These merchant and customer figures use different units. Do not add them to claim 29,500 unique people or assume every merchant represents one individual.

As of the November 27 notice, Octopus reports no detected customer financial losses and says card and wallet balances remain protected. These are the company’s current findings, not an independent final forensic conclusion. Its main services remain operational: customers can continue using physical cards, Mobile Octopus and Octopus Wallet. A small number of features may be temporarily affected by preventive measures; the notice does not name them or announce a complete service shutdown.

The company says it has informed relevant authorities, is investigating comprehensively and will update its assessment as findings develop. Affected customers are to receive direct email or SMS notices. It does not disclose the entry point, authentication prerequisites, a vulnerability identifier, affected software versions or public attack indicators. The merchant verification step above is precautionary, not a report that attackers have already redirected payments. L4 reflects the confirmed cyberattack and the breadth of the two affected data groups; no stolen-money total or completed recovery is asserted.
