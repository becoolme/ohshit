checklist:
- title: Ask which of your benefits records were involved
  hint: Use your established insurer, employer benefits administrator or Doctor Anywhere support channel. Income’s possible list includes full name, email, phone, NRIC/FIN/passport number, address, birth date, gender, nationality, race, emergency contacts, policy details and bank name/account number. This is its administrator-reported possible scope, not a universal field list for all Doctor Anywhere users.
- title: Watch banking and policy-related impersonation
  hint: Monitor bank accounts and contact the bank immediately over suspicious activity. Avoid unexpected links, attachments and QR codes; never disclose passwords or one-time codes to a purported insurer or benefits administrator. Income directs suspected policy impersonation to 6788 1777 and scams to 1799 or ScamShield; verify these through its official site.
- title: Follow direct notices without assuming medical records were stolen
  hint: Keep your notice and ask about your specific records. Income reports no evidence of medical-record access or its own system compromise, while Doctor Anywhere says clinical records were unaffected and services remain operational. These are investigation-stage assessments, not an independent guarantee. No public customer patch or blanket password reset is specified.

The primary notice concerns a third-party administrator’s Doctor Anywhere system, and does not establish a breach of Income’s own infrastructure. Its possible fields should not be generalized to all patients or policyholders. No victim count, intrusion mechanism or confirmed fraud total is published. L4 reflects confirmed unauthorized access involving sensitive personal records, with final scope unresolved.
