The Korean venues use Accesso’s ticketing system for visitor reservations. They say an unauthorized party viewed some annual pass records containing names, encrypted dates of birth, addresses, email addresses, phone numbers and pass barcodes. The birth dates were encrypted at rest. The notices say those fields are still being assessed and report no evidence of exposure of credit card or bank account details.

According to the Korean venues, Accesso removed the vulnerable endpoint, blocked the source addresses and tightened security rules. The exact time of the underlying access, number of people affected and which customers’ data was taken remain under investigation. Those Korean notices cover their own customers and give no numbers for Accesso’s other customers.


**Japan scope, disclosed November 18 and checked November 27.** Japan lists names, birth dates, gender, addresses, email, phone and annual-pass barcodes. Unlike Korea, its notice does not specify birth-date encryption. Accesso removed the vulnerable connection and applied fixes; external investigation continues. Japan’s FAQ identifies the Asia-Pacific Passport platform and says the affected functionality is fixed. Its payment-data finding does not establish every field’s safety. The notices do not establish identical exploits or access times across venues.
