If accessed, the user database could expose usernames, email addresses, IP addresses and password hashes. The operator warns that hashing does not fully protect weak or reused passwords. AO3 does not collect real names. The operator says donor information is held in a separate system, is never linked to AO3 and was not exposed. The investigation has not established the entry point, affected-account count or database theft. L3 reflects a major platform’s confirmed security disruption, rather than a confirmed mass data leak.


**November 6 update:** At that point, AO3 said restoration and changes to prevent future incidents were proceeding well and the original return estimate still applied. Some subscription/kudos notices queued before the October outage were sent during recovery; the operator confirms those messages are legitimate. The team says it is actively working to prevent drafts from being deleted. Those notices alone did not confirm reopening or completed draft preservation; the later notices below establish service recovery and preservation while leaving the database-exposure investigation unresolved.

**November 15–19 recovery:** AO3’s November 15 statement confirms the site is available with restrictions. Icons are hidden rather than deleted, external work importing remains unavailable, and published works can only be downloaded as HTML. The operator says works, bookmarks, kudos and other AO3 data have not been deleted, and confirms draft deletion was paused successfully with no drafts deleted while the site was down. Invitation emails and account creation resumed November 17. After another preventative-maintenance interruption on November 19, AO3 again announced that the site was up with the same restricted features. The latest recovery notice describes loading delays as intentional traffic management; a return notice does not promise uninterrupted service or restoration of every feature.

**Save drafts before November 29.** The November 15 statement extends the draft-deletion pause for two weeks, then schedules the normal process to resume November 29 and delete drafts that have reached 30 days. The date displayed on a draft remains based on its original creation date and does not show the temporary extension. The operator gives no resumption hour or timezone. Save local copies or publish drafts ahead of the date rather than treating the pause as indefinite or relying on the displayed deadline. This is a planned return to normal draft retention, not a report that security-incident data was deleted.

AO3 says it has partnered with a security firm to review the incident and strengthen the site. Its recovery statement still says it has no reason to believe the user database was compromised but cannot rule that out. It recommends considering an AO3 password change, checking the account email and changing passwords reused elsewhere. Service recovery and preserved drafts do not settle the entry point, exposed-account count or database-exposure question; no completed forensic report is provided in these notices.
