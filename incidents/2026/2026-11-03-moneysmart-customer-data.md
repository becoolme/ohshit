The reports attribute the confirmed data copying and containment to MoneySmart. The company says services continue and independent specialists are investigating. No affected-customer total, intrusion mechanism or complete list of exposed fields has been published in the reviewed material. Do not infer that passwords or card numbers were taken, or that those categories have been ruled out.

The Straits Times, read via its public ANN copy in The Star, additionally lists potentially accessed birth dates, nationality, identification numbers, mobile numbers, income bands, life stages, owned financial products, vehicle details and car-insurance inquiries/policies. These categories come from that report; they are not confirmed for every customer. It reports notices sent on November 2 and 3. Ask MoneySmart about your own records.

L4 reflects confirmed unauthorized access and data copying, rather than a proven mass-victim count. There is no customer software patch. Follow individual notices and the [official incident page](https://www.moneysmart.sg/security-check), which was inaccessible during this check; its contents were not independently read.
